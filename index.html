Section 1

Conviction & the cultural moment

Chuck Colson argued the Church had embraced the Great Commission but largely neglected the cultural commission — the call to engage, restore, and reconcile culture itself.

We are less interested in a victory story than in how you weigh cost, wisdom, and faithfulness.

Knowing the edge of one's competence is itself a governance strength.

Section 2

Our mission & legacy

Our work reaches the Church through Breakpoint, the Colson Fellows, Colson Educators, What Would You Say?, the Identity Project, Truth Rising, and the Institute for Christian Anthropology — often alongside partners such as Alliance Defending Freedom and the Association of Christian Schools International.

Candor is welcome. We would rather hear a real critique now than a courteous one later.

Section 3

Governance judgment

Our board governs — setting direction, guarding mission fidelity and financial integrity, and supporting the CEO — as distinct from managing the ministry's work. Members serve on a volunteer basis and contribute through committees such as Executive, Audit & Finance, and Theology.

Section 4

Capacity & commitment

Our board is national and meets in person approximately three times per year, with committee work and preparation between meetings. We would rather know your true capacity now than discover it later.

Can you commit to approximately three in-person meetings per year, including travel?*

A candid answer here is a mark of maturity, not a disqualifier.

Section 5

Generosity & advocacy

As an ECFA-accredited ministry, we ask every board member to be a personal financial supporter — giving meaningfully in proportion to their means — and to open doors as an ambassador for the work.

Do you embrace the expectation that board members give personally and meaningfully, according to their ability?*
