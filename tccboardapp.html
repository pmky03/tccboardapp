    Board of Directors — Candidate Screening

    Thank you for prayerfully considering board service with the Colson Center for Christian Worldview. Chuck Colson founded this ministry on a conviction that still drives us: that Christianity is not merely a private relationship with Jesus, or even personal discipleship, but a way of seeing and comprehending all reality — and that every Christian can, and should, live like one. Every single day.

    "Christianity is more than a relationship with Jesus. It is more than discipleship. It is a way of seeing and comprehending all reality."

    Chuck Colson, Founder

    The Colson Center exists to equip Christians with the clarity, confidence, and courage to live out a Christian worldview in this cultural moment — taking up the "cultural commission" the Church has too often neglected, and forming believers to be restorers and reconcilers in every sphere of life, often alongside partners such as Alliance Defending Freedom and the Association of Christian Schools International.

    Clarity

    A foundation for understanding all reality — God's story, our place in it, and how to live as a result.

    Confidence

    Connecting that foundation to the real situations and questions believers face in life and culture.

    Courage

    Equipping Christians to pursue their vocation and callings with wisdom, so they can serve and impact others.

    This confidential screening helps our Board Development Committee discern fit across three dimensions: shared faith and doctrine, alignment with the Colson Center's mission and worldview, and governance gifting and capacity.

    10 sections · ~20–30 minutes Confidential — committee review only You may complete in one sitting

    Faith & Spiritual Life

    How you came to faith, and what your walk with Christ looks like today.

    Pastoral / spiritual reference

    A pastor or spiritual leader who can speak to your character and walk with Christ. We may contact this person.

    Section 3

    Doctrinal Alignment

    Board members must wholeheartedly affirm the Colson Center Statement of Faith. These convictions — on the authority of Scripture, the imago Dei, human dignity and embodiment, marriage, and the gospel — are not boilerplate; they are the foundation of everything we teach. Please read each article and affirm. If you hold any reservation, you will have space to explain below.

    If none, simply write "No reservations." We welcome candor.

    Section 4

    Worldview & Cultural Engagement

    Worldview is the heart of the Colson Center. We help believers see that their stories have meaning because they are part of God's Story, and we equip them to answer life's deepest questions through a biblical lens — where we came from and who we are, what has gone wrong, and what can be done to make it right. This conviction runs through Breakpoint, the Colson Fellows, Colson Educators, What Would You Say?, the Identity Project, Truth Rising, and the Institute for Christian Anthropology.

    We're less interested in a textbook definition than in how you actually see the world.

    Section 5

    Governance Experience & Competencies

    The Colson Center's board brings skill and diversity to the ministry's work, and its members serve on a volunteer basis. A healthy board contributes complementary gifts through committees such as the Executive, Audit & Finance, and Theology committees — and understands that its role is to govern, distinct from the work of staff.

    Organizations served, your role (chair, treasurer, committee, etc.), nonprofit or for-profit, and years. If none, describe other leadership experience.

    Which competencies would you bring to the board? (Select all that apply)*
    A board member's primary role is governance — setting direction, ensuring mission fidelity and financial integrity, and supporting (not managing) the staff. How well does this match your understanding of board service?*
    Section 6

    Capacity, Commitment & Stewardship

    Board members serve on a volunteer basis, and the role asks for meaningful time, engaged participation, and personal generosity. We want to set clear, honest expectations.

    Are you able to commit to full participation — board meetings (typically several per year, in person and virtual), committee service, and preparation between meetings?*

    This helps us understand your overall capacity and avoid over-commitment.

    The Colson Center asks that board members support the ministry as personal financial givers, to the best of their ability, and serve as ambassadors. Is this an expectation you embrace?*
